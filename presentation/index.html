History

Three needs. Three places. Until now.


Pages & Pour was created from a common problem experienced by students and readers: finding one place where they can buy books, enjoy coffee, and study comfortably. Students often have to move between different locations. A bookstore to buy school materials, a café to get food and drinks, and a library or study space to work. Libraries are quiet but you can't eat or talk. Coffee shops are fine for a drink but not for hours of studying. Bookstores don't let you sit at all.

Pages & Pour brings these needs together in one location. Instead of spending time traveling between different places, customers can stay in one comfortable environment where they can study, read, work, socialize, and enjoy their favorite drinks and snacks.

Bookstore + Café + Study space = Pages & Pour

Students often have to move between a bookstore to buy school materials, a café for food and drinks, and a library or study space to work. Pages & Pour brings these needs together in one location.

Mission

To provide students, readers, and young professionals with a comfortable and convenient place to read, study, work, and relax while enjoying a nice coffee and having convenient access to books and learning materials without having to go somewhere else for each one.

Vision

To become the go-to neighborhood hub for students and readers, a place beyond home and school. Known for its books, brews, and its productive and welcoming atmosphere.

Competitor analysis

Each one covers only a single need.

Competitor type Example Strength Weakness (our opportunity)
Traditional bookstores National Book Store, local bookshops Lots of books to choose from No place to sit, read, or study
Coffee shops Local cafés, chain coffee shops Good ambiance, a nice place to sit No books, and can be noisy or not study-friendly
Coworking / study spaces Study cafés, libraries Quiet, made for studying No books to buy, often limited food options, can feel plain
Libraries School and public libraries Quiet and suitable for studying Food and drinks may not be allowed, limited access, and strict rules

Our edge

Pages & Pour is the only one that does all three. No need to go to a bookstore, then a coffee shop, then a library.

Walk the table row by row: each competitor type is strong at one thing and weak at the others. Close on our edge: Pages & Pour is the only one that does all three.

Primary logo

The coffee-ring seal.

Pages (the book) and Pour (the cup) become a single icon: a mug resting on a volume, steam rising like a turning chapter.

The frame is a coffee ring, the stain left on a page after a long sit.

This is our primary logo. Point out the cup on the book and the coffee-ring frame.

Secondary logos

One mark, different sizes.

The pictorial mark works for small spaces like icons and stamps. The badge lockup adds EST. 2026 and the tagline.

From sketch to logo

How the mark was made.

  1. 01Each member sketched logo concepts on their own.
  2. 02We shared the drafts in our group chat and compared them for recurring elements, themes, and layouts.
  3. 03Several feedback rounds blended everyone’s ideas into draft variations instead of picking one person’s drawing. Weak concepts dropped out.
  4. 04Lock the cup-on-book pictorial from the 3-in-1 idea: bookstore, café, study spot. Put the name on the mug so the icon still reads at small sizes.
  5. 05Build a badge lockup with the tagline “One Place. Every Page. Every Pour.” and a leaf divider.
  6. 06Finish with a coffee-ring seal as the brand frame, a logo every member contributed to and the whole group stands behind.
Show both sketches: the cup-on-book mark first, then the badge lockup with the tagline. Walk the four steps from sketch to final seal.
