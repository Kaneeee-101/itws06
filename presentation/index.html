History

Three needs. Three places. Until now.

Pages & Pour was created from a common problem experienced by students and readers: finding one place where they can buy books, enjoy coffee, and study comfortably.

Bookstore+Café+Study space=Pages & Pour
Students often have to move between a bookstore to buy school materials, a café for food and drinks, and a library or study space to work. Pages & Pour brings these needs together in one location.

Mission

To provide students, readers, and young professionals with a comfortable and convenient place to read, study, work, and relax while enjoying a nice coffee and having convenient access to books and learning materials without having to go somewhere else for each one.

Vision

To become the go-to neighborhood hub for students and readers, a place beyond home and school. Known for its books, brews, and its productive and welcoming atmosphere.

Read the mission, then the vision. Emphasize “a place beyond home and school.”

Value proposition

One space for three needs: buying books, getting coffee, and finding a comfortable place to study.

It saves time and money because customers do not need to visit three different locations.

  1. 01Quiet ZoneFor individual studying and reading.
  2. 02Group ZoneFor discussions and group projects.
  3. 03Reading LoungeFor casual reading and relaxation.
  4. 04Creative CornerFor writing, journaling, sketching, and school projects.
Pages & Pour is a 3-in-1 space. Its study zones, books, affordable food and drinks, Wi-Fi, charging outlets, printing, and creative space make it more than a place to drink coffee.

Creative Task #1

Logo Design

From two needs, one mark.

Start the logo design part.

Primary logo

The coffee-ring seal.

Pages (the book) and Pour (the cup) become a single icon: a mug resting on a volume, steam rising like a turning chapter.

The frame is a coffee ring, the stain left on a page after a long sit.

This is our primary logo. Point out the cup on the book and the coffee-ring frame.

Secondary logos

One mark, different sizes.

The pictorial mark works for small spaces like icons and stamps. The badge lockup adds EST. 2026 and the tagline.

From sketch to logo

How the mark was made.

  1. 01Start with the 3-in-1 idea: bookstore, café, study spot — one object that can carry all three.
  2. 02Lock the cup-on-book pictorial. Put the name on the mug so the icon still reads at small sizes.
  3. 03Build a badge lockup with the tagline “One Place. Every Page. Every Pour.” and a leaf divider.
  4. 04Finish with a coffee-ring seal as the brand frame.
Show both sketches: the cup-on-book mark first, then the badge lockup with the tagline. Walk the four steps from sketch to final seal.
